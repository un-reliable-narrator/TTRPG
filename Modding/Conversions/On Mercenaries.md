# On Mercenaries

In a "Mount and Blade"-style campaign, one needs rules on how to raise a mercenary army. For starters, consider the [[Hirelings]] table, as well as my [[Militia Training]] document. Also, take a look at [[Lifestyle Expenses]] and [[food-drink-and-lodging|Food, Drink, and Lodging]].

## Mercenaries vs. Hirelings

In a typical DnD campaign, a Hireling can reasonably expect:
- To work with a small crew of rich, heroic superpowered adventurers. They probably know each of their names.
- To have those adventurers handle the most dangerous and violent tasks.
- To be able to terminate their employment and return home at any time, without repercussion.
By contrast, a typical mercenary recruit (i.e. a peasant youth) should expect:
- To work with a large band of professional killers.
- To do the brunt of the fighting and heavy lifting.
- To join long-term, contractually obligated under threat of death (for desertion).
- To leave home and possibly never return.

It stands to reason, then, that terms and conditions will differ.

## Payment

It's widely considered reasonable for companies to pay their recruits an upfront recruitment fee equal to 10x their daily wage. 

| Recruitment                                                                                | Fee          | Daily Wage  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------ | ----------- |
| Villager/Peasant (Commoner) (cannot have a statblock with CR higher than 0)                | 2 GP         | 2 SP        |
| Citizens and Warriors (Wealthy/skilled Commoners, any humanoid statblock with max. 1/2 CR) | 20 GP        | 2 GP        |
| Creatures with a statblock of 1+ CR                                                        | (40 x CR) GP | (4 x CR) GP |
**Higher/Lower Wages.** You are free to propose wages of your choice, which may hamper or benefit your chances of finding willing recruits. For example, a higher initial fee may grant you a reroll on the number of recruits, as per DM discretion.

**Looting.** Mercenaries expect to take their share of the plunder, and will usually make use of this right at the first opportunity. You may want to create some minimal rules, in order to keep casualties over loot disputes at a minimum.

## Recruitment

Entering a community and trying to rustle up recruits is not a guaranteed success. To determine if and how many interested recruits you find, make a performance check (DC dependent on location).

| Location              | DC  | Willing Recruits    | (*Recruit Health*) |
| --------------------- | --- | ------------------- | ------------------ |
| Village (pillaged)    | 5   | 1d4 Commoners       | Low                |
| Village (destitute)   | 10  | 2d6 Commoners       | Low                |
| Village (comfortable) | 15  | 2d6 Commoners       | Medium             |
| Monastery             | 20  | 1d4 Priest Acolytes |                    |
|                       |     |                     |                    |
*Optional Rule:* If you assign size categories to locations, scale the number of dice of Willing Recruits with settlement size.

## Rations

A ration costs 5 SP, and feeds a single medium humanoid for 1 day. Larger creatures may consume more food. (Though certain species, as well as beasts like horses, may benefit from reduced consumption, through grazing or otherwise.)

| Creature Size | Daily Rations |
| ------------- | ------------- |
| Tiny          | 1/4           |
| Small         | 1             |
| Medium        | 1             |
| Large         | 2             |
| Huge          | 4             |
| Gargantuan    | 16            |
## "Gatefall"

As sailors celebrate their day in port, road-weary mercenaries long for a stay in a friendly city. As company leader, you'd be advised to make arrangements for lodging and a meal. 

## Morale

Food, Lodging, etc. affects Morale. More/better food can help.
